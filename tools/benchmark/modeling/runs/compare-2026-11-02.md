## Reading

- **Both changes help a little, and they add up, but the ceiling barely moves.** The mean went from 2.65 to 2.85; the likeness-4 answers went from 3 to 5.
- **The gains are where an idiom matched one bold arrangement.** That was the Pompidou frame, the Panthéon's lantern and windowless walls, the Parc des Princes ribs and the Hôtel de Ville court.
- **The hard failures did not move at all,** even where v2 states the exact idiom: the Palace of Fine Arts rotunda, the Legion court, SFMOMA, Garnier, the Asian Art Museum, de Young. Haiku keeps defaulting to "one block plus one feature".
- **Feedback rarely produces a structural rethink.** Haiku's revisions keep the draft's layout and add or adjust parts. It doubles the cost for a small gain.
- **Single-shot invalid answers (1 in 20 per run) are noise** that `--attempts=2` would remove.

## Verdict: both, and the setup is the binding limit

- **The model gap is real.** Sonnet reads arrangements Haiku does not: the Legion court, SFMOMA's stepped symmetry and striped oculus, the Ferry arcade, the L-shaped BnF towers. It lifts the floor: 19 of 20 recognisable against 14.
- **But the ceiling barely moves.** Sonnet, at 20× the price, lands at "recognisable, with clear errors" on 12 of 20, at 4 on 7, and at 5 on none. A setup that tops out near 3.3 for Sonnet will not get Haiku to "good enough" by prompting.
- **Where the setup caps everyone:**
  - **Facades.** A third of the set is Beaux-Arts (Garnier, Hôtel de Ville, Orsay, the Asian Art Museum, Gare de Lyon). Their identity is in pavilions, pilasters, mansards with dormers, cornices and rustication. The DSL's facade vocabulary is four window patterns, so every model renders them as office blocks with grey lids. The game's own kit has this grammar (`facade()` slots, aedicules, quoins, mansards with dormers); the benchmark's language does not.
  - **Forms with no primitive.** There is no twist (de Young), no open drum (the Palace of Fine Arts fails for both models even with the idiom spelled out), and no stepped or curved roof.
  - **Brittle validation.** Four of the 80 Haiku answers and 3 of Sonnet's 20 failed on degenerate values (radius 0, zero height, an unknown material) that could safely be clamped or defaulted with a warning.

## v3: a facade grammar, larger parts, lenient checking

**v3 added:**
- `block`: a facade grammar. Storeys from the ground up (rusticated, arcade, columns, pilasters, arched…), string courses, a cornice, and a roof (mansard with dormers, pavilion, dome…).
- `court`, `rotunda` (open or closed) and `tower` (stages, clock, belfry, spire, dome or cupola).
- `twist`, `ring` and `disc`.
- Idioms rewritten around the new parts.
- Lenient checking: values clamped, materials mapped, broken parts dropped with a warning.

The macros expand to the old parts, so rendering and scoring are unchanged. Sheet: [sidebyside-v3-front34.jpg](sidebyside-v3-front34.jpg), with columns photo, Haiku v2-fb, Haiku v3, Sonnet v2, Sonnet v3. Both v3 runs are single-shot, with no feedback turn.

**Invalid answers.** One per model, neither a slip that leniency was built for:
- Haiku's Palace of Fine Arts was one bare `rotunda` object with no `{"parts": [...]}` wrapper. Lenient mode now accepts that, but only from this run on; this run keeps it as invalid.
- Sonnet's Parc des Princes put a JavaScript expression in the JSON.

Leniency dropped only 3 parts across both runs.

### Reading

- **v3 gives Haiku what the feedback turn gave it, at half the price.** That is 2.90 at $0.0015 against 2.85 at $0.0030. Sonnet edges up to 3.10 on a strict count.
- **The ceiling still does not move.** Neither model reaches likeness 5 anywhere, and most answers sit at 3.

- **The bottleneck has moved.** The vocabulary is no longer missing; what fails now is choosing the right part and the right settings for each building from the photographs.

## The bar: the game's own models, through the same renderer

Sheets: [sidebyside-reference-front34.jpg](sidebyside-reference-front34.jpg) and [sidebyside-reference-rear34.jpg](sidebyside-reference-rear34.jpg), with columns photo, game reference, Haiku v3, Sonnet v3.

[reference.ts](../reference.ts) builds each building's game builder (mostly Opus-made) on its own footprint, exactly as `tools/model-shots.ts` does. It then draws the result in viewer.html with flat vertex colours, from the same three views, with no textures and no lit windows. Each mesh is turned onto the benchmark outline by footprint IoU, never mirrored, since both worlds are x east, z north.

Where a plan is symmetric, a reference can face a different side from the contestants. The Madeleine is seen from the rear; Garnier and Gare de Lyon are turned. That changes which side is visible, not the likeness.

### What "good enough" looks like here

- **The bar is about 4 in this renderer, and 5 is reachable.** Flat colours do not cap likeness. The game's models reach 4 almost everywhere, and 5 on the Parc des Princes, the Panthéon and the Sacré-Cœur, with nothing but geometry and vertex colour. So "good enough" means likeness 4: the arrangement right (court, pavilions, loggia, open rotunda), proportions right, roofs the right form and colour, and enough repeated detail (window rows, columns, ribs, skylights) to read at a glance.
- **The contestants sit about a point below it.** Sonnet matches or beats the reference on 6 of 19 buildings: Oracle Park, de Young, Ferry Building, Pompidou, Madeleine and SFMOMA, the last only because the game's own SFMOMA is wrong. Haiku does so on 3: Madeleine, plus the two where the reference is weak.
- **The gap is arrangement and density, not vocabulary.** The references carry about five times the geometry (median 9.7k triangles against about 2k), and where they win it is composition: the Legion court, the open Palace of Fine Arts, Garnier's stage house behind the dome, the Sacré-Cœur campanile. A contestant with v3's parts could say all of these. It doesn't, because it doesn't choose them.
- **The benchmark found a bad reference.** The game's SFMOMA (`sfmoma_151_third`) does not look like the Botta building in the photographs. It is worth a look in the game itself, with its textures, before concluding it is wrong there too.

## Closing the gap for Haiku: plan, then build; the full stack; best of 3

Sheets: [sidebyside-v4-front34.jpg](sidebyside-v4-front34.jpg) (bar, Haiku v3, v4 plan, v4 full, Sonnet v3) and [sidebyside-best-front34.jpg](sidebyside-best-front34.jpg) (bar, v4 full, best of 3, Sonnet v3).

**Changes, all measured on Haiku:**
- **Plan, then build** (`--plan=plan-v2`). A first call ([prompt/plan-v2.md](../prompt/plan-v2.md)) names the signature features and picks the larger part, position, size and settings for each, with explicit checks ("courtyard? use `court`"; "sky through it? it is open"). The build call gets the plan and must build every component.
- **v4 prompt:** v3 plus two parts the test runs showed were missing:
  - `rotunda` with `arches`: great open arches between piers, with paired columns.
  - Storeys with `"windows": false`, for blank walls.

  The first test Palace of Fine Arts plan said "sky visible through the arches" and still picked a closed drum, because nothing else fitted.
- **Full stack** (`v4-full`): plan, then build at effort high, a repair turn (`--attempts=2`) and the render-feedback turn.
- **Best of 3** (`v4-plan-x3-best`): three plan-and-build samples per building, rendered. A Haiku call then picks the one closest to the photos from the front-right and top views ([select.ts](../select.ts)). The cost counts all three candidates and the selector.

### Reading

- **Haiku now matches Sonnet at a third of the cost.** The stack lifts it from 2.90 to about 3.1, every answer is valid, and the arrangement failures are fixed: the court, the open rotunda, the clock towers, the frame. That is Sonnet v3's level for $0.008–0.009 a building against $0.024.
- **It is still about a point under the bar, and nothing reaches 5.** The steps add up but flatten out: plan +0.1, the full stack +0.1, best of 3 +0.05. The selector mostly picked well; its choices were never worse than the alternatives on the sheet.
- **What still separates a 3 from the bar's 4:**
  - Office-style punched windows on walls that have none: the Sacré-Cœur's base, the Panthéon's arms, Garnier. `windows: false` exists but is rarely used.
  - Proportions: egg-shaped domes, a stubby de Young tower.
  - About a quarter of the bar's geometric density.
- **Confidence.** One rater (Claude), one sample per configuration except best of 3, with half-point judgements. Differences under about 0.15 are within noise; the climb from 2.65 (v1) to 3.15 is not.
