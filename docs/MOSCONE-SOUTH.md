# The Moscone South block — what it actually is

Established 2026-09-23 from the owner's aerial obliques, vector views and description, **supplied BEFORE any
modelling began**. That is the difference between this block and Yerba Buena, where three runs reconstructed
from street frames what an aerial showed directly (BUGS.md #132).

Bounded by **Howard (north), 3rd (east), Folsom (south), 4th (west)**. It is the twin of the Yerba Buena
block across Howard, and it is similarly complicated.

## THE LEVEL MODEL

Same two-level logic as the north block, and the same trap: **most of what stands here is on the deck, not on
the ground.**

- **Level 1 — Moscone South hall.** Occupies **most of the block**. This is the ground the rest sits on.
- **Level 2 — garden/deck level.** The **grassy part only** — the lawn at the south end by the Public Square.
- **Level 2.5 — a MEZZANINE, and MOST of the park is on it.** Half a storey above level 2. It exists to give
  **depth for the ponds**: the Children's Park, its pool, its big pond and the oval grass all sit up here.
- **Level 2.75 — the top of the curved dividing wall**, a little above the mezzanine.
- **Level 3 — the Children's Creativity Museum's second floor** (third counting the Moscone hall below).

### How the levels connect — the curved wall is the hinge

A **curved wall structure divides the two garden areas**. Its top is level 2.75, and from it:

- **two curved esplanades** descend on one side to **level 2** (the lawn), and
- **a concrete ramp** descends to **level 2.5** (the mezzanine).

Separately, a **big curved walkway leaves the Children's Creativity Museum's second level (level 3)** and
**descends a curved esplanade to level 2.5**.

So the block has **five distinct walkable heights** and the transitions between them are curved, not
straight. This is the single hardest thing on the block and it is the thing that makes the park legible.

### LEVEL 3 IS A CONNECTED NETWORK, not isolated roof-tops

The most important structural fact about this block, and easy to miss. Level 3 is not a set of separate
upper floors — **it is a continuous upper circulation system**:

    Children's Creativity Museum (level 3)
        → connection across to the CHILD CARE / "brutalist" building (level 3)
        → walkway on to the ICE RINK
        → and a separate stair DOWN to garden level

So a person can arrive at the museum's upper floor and walk, at height, right across the west and south of
the block before coming down. Combined with the curved walkway that leaves the museum's level 3 and descends
to level 2.5, **the upper level has at least three separate ways down**.

This is the Yerba Buena skybridge idea again, but as a *network* rather than two single crossings — and it is
the strongest argument yet that this block's value to a rider is its **vertical circulation**, not its
buildings. Model the links, not just the masses.

**Unlike the north side, Moscone here is NOT buried.** Two large above-ground halls rise well clear of the
deck, and they dominate the block's edges.

## Going round the block

### Howard side (north) — the big hall. **ALSO A SIMPLE BUILD.**

A **huge above-ground section of Moscone, 6–7 levels high**. The largest single mass on the block and what
the Yerba Buena gardens look across Howard at.

**CORRECTION 2026-09-23 — I wrote here that both skybridges land on this side. MEASURED, THEY DO NOT.**
Neither lands on `way/426708093`. In city world metres the tube lands at (938.9, −1004.8) and the open deck
at (879.7, −1025.7), while this hall's Howard edge runs (1012.5, −913.4) → (956.4, −970.2) — the tube is
~39 m beyond its south-west corner, the open deck ~110 m. **Both land on `way/109141776`**, the way that
carries the `mosconeHall` preset and is capped by `mosconeSouthDeck`. So the "break where the skybridge
lands" does not belong on this building's glazed band.

Despite its size the form is straightforward — one long slab with a strong horizontal layering, and it is
**sayable in the kit**:

- a **deep, dark, open UNDERCROFT** at street level — the mass is lifted, and you see through and under it.
  This is the most striking thing about it from the pavement and the easiest to get wrong by drawing a solid
  wall to the ground;
- above that, a **long continuous band of blue-green glazing**, slightly recessed, running nearly the full
  length, with fine vertical mullions and a break where the skybridge lands;
- a **white parapet/roof edge** carrying a **long horizontal louvre or fin band** the whole length;
- **large solar arrays** across the flat roof behind — visible from the north block's decks and from both
  bridges, so worth drawing.

**It is NOT one box — it is THREE sub-buildings**, stepping in plan and height along Howard, still
extremely boxy. Build it as three masses that share a datum, not as one slab. The 4th Street end carries a
**pale perforated/translucent screen** over its glazing — a large fine chequer — quite different from the
blue-green band further along.

### THE TWO SKYBRIDGES LAND DIFFERENTLY — this is the answer to a long-open question

They are **not** a matched pair, and neither lands where I earlier assumed:

- **One goes INTO the building** — it enters Moscone's upper floor directly and stops.
- **The other lands at WALL LEVEL (2.75)** — the top of the curved dividing wall, out in the open.

**From that wall you can then descend either to the Children's Park (2.5) or to the grassy circle park
(2).** So one bridge delivers you indoors, and the other delivers you onto the hinge of the whole park with
a choice of two descents.

That second one is the ride-worthy route: cross Howard in the air from the north block, arrive on a wall
above a park, and choose your way down. It is the single best piece of circulation on either block.

The second bridge is also the one Google Earth's mesh omits (#139).

**Two of this block's biggest masses — this hall and the ice rink — are therefore cheap.** Build them first:
between them they fix the deck height, both long frontages and where the bridges land, which everything
harder on the block has to meet.

### 3rd Street side (east) — the curved entrance
Another **large above-ground Moscone hall**, with a **very interesting curved front entrance / esplanade** —
a long shallow **arc** cut into the frontage, with **stepped seating/terraces** following the curve, planted
beds along it, and a **glazed canopy carried on a row of slender columns with exposed diagonal bracing**
running the length of the arc. Stairs descend through it to street level.

**This is the block's set piece.** It is the one thing here a rider will remember, it is geometrically
distinctive, and it is well photographed from above. Do not reduce it to a flat wall.

### Folsom side (south)
- the far side of the second Moscone hall;
- the **Yerba Buena Ice Skating Center**, a large boxy building;
- **an enormous staircase between the ice rink and the second Moscone hall** — a major piece of circulation,
  not a detail. A near-overhead oblique shows it properly, and it is **BROAD, not narrow** — an earlier,
  more distant frame made it look like a thin flight and that reading was wrong.

  It is a **wide monumental flight**, many metres across, running the full depth from **Folsom Street
  straight up to garden level** in one continuous run, in the canyon between the Ice Skating Center (west,
  silver ribbed roof) and the second Moscone hall (east). **A broad plain paved strip runs parallel to it**
  on the west side — either a ramp or a plain walk — so the ascent is a **stair-and-walk pair**, not a
  single object.

  The Moscone hall wall facing it is strongly articulated: **big white concrete piers** at regular bays with
  **stepped planted terraces** between them, stepping up alongside the flight.

  This is a genuine set piece — a monumental public stair the full depth of a city block. Model the pair and
  the pier-and-terrace wall beside it, not just treads.
- the **pink building at garden level** is **The Veranda San Francisco**, an events venue standing on top of
  the level-1 Moscone hall. Salmon-pink rendered parapet walls, flat dark roof, plainly boxy with a stepped
  plan; roof plant in a pink-walled enclosure at its north end; a **curved pale canopy** at its south end
  over the entrance, and a low curved wall below that. The colour is the point — it is the only pink
  building anywhere near either block and it will read instantly from the decks.
- the **Ice Skating Center** (`way/27572497`) beside it — **the easiest building on this block**, and the
  only easy one. The owner: *"it's basically a box with a smaller box on top and a roof."*

  A large box, a **smaller box set on top of it**, and a **pale silver standing-seam metal roof** with a
  strong fine rib over the whole thing. A tall **blank cream-coloured wall** at its west end, and lower
  white service blocks along its base.

  **This is sayable in the kit** — a mass, a set-back mass above it, a ribbed roof. It does not need
  scratch modelling. The queue has it at `manual` (carried over from before this block was understood);
  it can be done cheaply and should not absorb scratch-tier effort that the carousel, the curved esplanade
  and the child care centre genuinely need.

  Useful as a **first build on this block**: it establishes the deck height, the Folsom frontage and the
  canyon's west wall, all of which the monumental stair beside it has to meet.

### 4th and Folsom corner — the "brutalist" building, identified

It is **`relation/3832310`, South of Market Child Care** (the Child Development Center) — the 116-corner
organic plan the surroundings audit flagged as not kit-modelable and never queued. A zoomed oblique shows it
clearly, and it is far stranger than "a brutalist box":

- **Low, one to two storeys**, on garden level, sprawling across the whole Folsom frontage in **several
  offset wings**.
- **Pale standing-seam metal roofs** with a strong fine rib, on each wing — and the wings' roof edges and
  parapets **UNDULATE**: wavy, not straight. That curve is the building's signature.
- **Round skylight drums** set into the roofs — four or five pale cones/cylinders, plainly visible from the
  decks above.
- **Board-formed concrete walls carrying large MURALS** along Folsom: figures and faces in strong colour,
  the only mural wall on either block.
- **White tensile canopies** over parts of the courtyards between wings.
- A **long planted bank** of shrubs along Folsom below the wall, with **one big straight staircase** cutting
  straight up through it from the pavement to garden level.

So the three things to get right are the **undulating roof edges**, the **round rooflights**, and the
**murals** — none of which any kit can say. The `stair()` part handles the Folsom flight.

**Murals are a backlog item with no support yet**: `SignSource {kind:'image',url}` is declared but dead code
(see the signage notes). This is the first building that actually needs it, rather than wanting it.

### 4th Street side (west)
- the **Children's Creativity Museum** (`way/288716388`) — on garden level, with **big stairs up into it**,
  and far stranger than "round drum forms". A close oblique shows the complex properly:
  - a **large DARK TRUNCATED CONE, wider at the top**, glazed at its lower drum — the museum's signature;
  - a **SERPENTINE building** curving in a long shallow S, banded in **orange/terracotta** over dark
    glazing, running from the Creativity Theater round to the cone;
  - the **Creativity Theater** at its west end under a dark curved roof;
  - between them a **CIRCULAR SUNKEN PLAZA** paved in pink-red, with a small planted island at its edge;
  - a **grand curved fan staircase** in pale stone sweeping up from that plaza to the upper level.

  A builder already exists (`childrensCreativityMuseum`, `model-opus.ts`) — **verify it against these
  aerials before trusting it**, because none of the serpentine, the sunken plaza or the fan stair is
  something a from-description model would have produced.
- the **LeRoy King Carousel** (`way/109141788`) at **4th and Howard**, on **TRUE GROUND LEVEL**, **taking a
  corner out of Moscone**. A close oblique corrects an earlier note here: the roof is **NOT conical**. It is
  a **shallow GLAZED DOME with radial ribs**, pale blue-green, sitting on a low drum with a decorative
  painted band, on a **circular plinth reached by steps all round**. The building starts at true ground
  level; **the carousel itself is raised on that platform**.

  Beside it stands a **large DARK TRUNCATED CONE, wider at the top than the bottom** — a dramatic inverted
  drum with a flat top and a small raised cap at its centre. It is the strongest single form in this corner
  and nothing in the kit can say it.

  The **Children's Creativity Museum connects DOWN to this corner**, and **several sets of stairs** climb
  from true ground level, or from the carousel's platform, up to garden level. Nearby retaining walls carry
  **angled buttress fins** in concrete.

  So this corner is where all three heights meet — true ground, the carousel platform, and garden level —
  and it is the busiest piece of vertical circulation on the block after the monumental Folsom stair.

### On the deck, centre
**Yerba Buena Public Square** is a semicircular **amphitheatre of concentric stepped grass terraces**,
quartered by cross paths — the strongest single geometric figure on either block, and unmistakable from
above.

**Yerba Buena Children's Garden** (level 2.5) holds a **large blue-green pond**, a **circular fountain plaza
in red paving**, a **play landscape** in pale blue and green surfacing, an **oval lawn** at its north end, and
dense planting along the 3rd Street edge.

Solar arrays cover large parts of the Moscone roofs either side — visible from every deck and worth drawing,
since a rider on this block looks across them.

## A WARNING ABOUT THE REFERENCE ITSELF

**Google Earth's 3D model is MISSING ONE OF THE TWO SKYBRIDGES.** The owner spotted it. Both exist in life;
the photographic aerial shows both, the textured 3D mesh shows one.

This is the first demonstrated case of our best new reference source being **wrong rather than merely
incomplete**, and it is the dangerous kind of wrong: a confident, photorealistic-looking model with a real
structure simply absent. An agent trusting it would have "verified" that only one bridge exists.

**Rule**: an aerial is evidence of what IS there, never proof of what is NOT. When the 3D mesh and a
photograph disagree, the photograph wins. Cross-check anything structural against a second view.

## What is NOT kit-modelable here

- the **carousel** — round, conical roof;
- the **3rd Street curved esplanade** — an arc, with curved stepped terraces and a braced canopy;
- the **amphitheatre** — concentric curved grass terraces;
- the **brutalist corner structure** — deeply articulated, stairs cut into the mass;
- the **Children's Creativity Museum** — drums and a dome;
- **Moscone itself** — geometry outside the footprint, multi-level, overhanging deck (BUGS.md #130).

Essentially the whole block is scratch work. That is expected: it is the twin of the hardest block in SF.
