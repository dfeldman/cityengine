# Buildings built from a recipe

**A one-line `fromRecipe(...)` builder is a GOOD outcome, not a defect.** The owner, 2026-09-24:

> *"the one line builders are fine! that's enough for most buildings! Don't dismiss those. It's fine. That
> means we looked at it and confirmed a style. It's no longer a generic or even merely restyled."*

A recipe call means a human or an agent looked at the building, identified its style, and committed to it.
That is a real rung on the ladder above generic classifier massing and above a bare restyle, and it is the
right answer for most of the city. **177 of 536 builders are recipe calls and that is a healthy proportion,
not a backlog.**

The narrow failure mode — and the only reason this list exists — is a recipe applied to a building whose
DEFINING FEATURE the recipe vocabulary cannot say. The Old San Francisco Mint was `midcentury`-style massing
when the whole point of the building is a giant Doric portico on a raised basement: no recipe in the set had
a portico, so the recipe could not have been right whatever parameters it was given. That is a kit-gap
problem, not a recipe problem, and the fix was to add `bankTemple()` to the kit.

So when scanning this list, ask only: **is this building's defining feature something the recipe vocabulary
has no word for?** — a portico, a dome, a curved or tapering plan, a giant order, an inverted-Y section.
If not, the recipe is the finished answer and the row needs nothing.
